THE PREMISE

The first casualty was not a law, a border, or an election.
It was a Sunday dinner.

Follow one neighborhood across the years as trust becomes suspicion, political disagreements become family betrayals, and a northern dictatorship discovers it can achieve more through a fractured society than through an army. The people think they are choosing sides. The psychologists are counting what disappears.

A NOTE ON THE WORLD

The map is imaginary.
The cost is human.

Liora, Veyr, their institutions, and every character in this book are fictional. The novel explores state interference, political polarization, platform incentives, and the private damage that public conflict can leave behind. Its narrative is not a claim that all real political disagreement is manufactured or that every dissenter suffers from, or lacks, a medical condition.

This is a novel without a compulsory happy ending. The people live with the consequences. The people who designed the campaign are rewarded.

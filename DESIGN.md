# THE DISTANCE BETWEEN HOUSES
## Novel and audiobook master design

**Form:** An original six-part, approximately 24-chapter contemporary political thriller with an epilogue, published as an audiobook-ready serial. Working length goal: 80,000–100,000 words. Parts are written and released in sequence. Parts I–V are written; Part VI and the epilogue below are a writing plan, **not published chapters**.

**Setting:** An invented, recognizably contemporary world. North and south are geographic poles in the story, not proxies that correspond exactly to any real nations. The northern dictatorship, **the Directorate of Veyr**, is conducting open military aggression in Tarven and other neighboring borderlands. Its covert political campaign is aimed at **the Republic of Liora**, a southern democracy that sanctions the aggressor and supports its neighbors. Liora loses its sovereign existence by the end of the book.

**Logline:** When a northern dictatorship discovers that it cannot easily invade a southern republic, a department of state psychologists studies the bonds that make the republic governable. Six years later the republic has vanished, those who tried to expose the program have been institutionalized, and the psychologists are awarded medals.

**No redemption mandate.** The novel ends with a victorious aggressor. There are acts of courage, but courage is not the same as victory. Awareness arrives too late to undo everything.

## The dramatic method

The novel alternates **human-scale consequence** (Rowan Way, schools, kitchens, hospitals, workplaces) and **institutional abstraction** (reports, diagrams, promotions, reassigned budgets, state honors). Every notable change should be legible in both scales. A line on a report becomes a child asking why his uncle no longer visits. A falling indicator becomes a library reading group with three empty chairs.

The northern operation is **deliberate, morally culpable, but not omnipotent**. It does not generate every real social grievance, control every algorithm, or know every citizen's mind. Veyr exploits conditions already present: an unfair housing market; an industrial downturn; legitimate disagreement over budgets; commercial attention systems; opportunistic local political entrepreneurs; stubbornness; fear. Its officials also overclaim credit, conceal uncertainty, and mistake correlations for causation. The danger is not magic persuasion, but a society that loses the habits needed to correct its mistakes.

The two increasingly hostile domestic coalitions acquire **real political differences**. Neither entire group is a monster. Individual acts can be cruel or brave; their consequences need not be symmetrical. The narrator never says that political difference itself is a disease. The irreversible injury is the abandonment of the shared institutions, relationships, and facts through which difference once remained survivable.

Mental illness is not a shorthand for wrongness. The later psychiatric detentions are **abuses of medical and state power**: administrative and legal processes are corrupted, contradictory evidence is suppressed, and individual clinicians face moral choices. Whistleblowers are not declared mentally ill merely because they correctly suspect foreign interference; the story dramatizes the machinery that falsely labels them.

## Character bible

- **Mara Arden**, school football organizer, married to Tomas, mother of Ivo. Competent, generous, frustrated that her local work is invisible. Public recognition becomes addictive; alienation initially feels like empowerment. She is never merely a gullible stereotype.
- **Tomas Arden**, railway signaling maintainer. A proud believer in public service who sometimes mistakes the righteousness of his cause for permission to hurt those near him. He knows how to maintain a railway signal and cannot repair a sentence said in anger.
- **Dain Arden**, Tomas's brother, municipal power technician. Values work, loyalty, independence. His grievances are often justified; online adulation turns his defensive pride into something more corrosive.
- **Ivo Arden**, their son. Starts at eleven. His understanding of politics is principally a growing map of rooms, people, and forbidden questions. At seventeen he must decide whether to accept identity papers issued by the authority that replaced his country.
- **Sela Orin**, librarian and former history teacher on Rowan Way. Begins by recording missing people in civic groups. Finds patterns, gathers evidence carefully, refuses speculative certainty. Later faces detention for insisting that the official account can be challenged.
- **Lev Orin**, Sela's wife, physician at the district clinic. Sees public hostility disrupt care. Her professional credibility is later weaponized against Sela, and she must choose between loyalty and the threat of losing her medical license.
- **Eva Maren**, regional journalist. Skeptical, methodical. Tracks payments, ownership, claims, and testimony; insists that suspicious patterns require evidence. Her investigation succeeds factually and fails politically.
- **Dr. Irena Solvik**, northern clinical psychologist, director of Civic Weather. Intelligent, meticulous, ambitious, capable of tenderness toward her family and coldness toward strangers. Her late knowledge of the human consequences does not absolve her; she chooses promotion over resistance.
- **Niko Rahl**, junior analyst whose technical caution becomes an ethical liability. Understands that association is not proof. Documents the gulf between what the operation claims and what it can actually establish. Whether his objections matter becomes part of the tragedy.
- **Director Kest**, senior northern state official. Not a genius manipulator; a dangerous bureaucrat who rewards confident reports and punishes unwelcome uncertainty.
- **Dr. Oren Pell**, Lioran psychiatrist introduced later. Initially honest about the difference between unpopular beliefs and delusions. Under emergency law, his assessments become instruments of detention. He must decide whether to sign.
- **Leaders of Liora's rival movements**, developed in Parts II–IV. They have local histories and independent agency. Some accept dishonest support; others do not know whose interests they serve. Their movements gradually become incentives that outlive the foreign funding.

## Continuity notes after Part III

- **Timeline:** Part I begins in late summer approximately six years before Liora's dissolution. Part II covers the following March–November and the second August supper. Part III runs from January through early May after that second supper. Exact calendar years are intentionally unstated in chapter headings.
- **Arden home:** Tomas moves to the small back room; he and Mara are still married and live at the same address, with Ivo observing the separation. They are not divorced or formally separated. Their mutual affection has not disappeared; their willingness to risk being misunderstood has.
- **Ivo and Tessa:** Both are around twelve, long-time friends since age five and football acquaintances. Their bridge project at school functions flawlessly after a repaired hinge, but their relationship is damaged following Civic Day. Ivo sends her `You were right about the hinge.`; Tessa sees it but does not reply. Do not turn this instantly into reconciliation.
- **Tarven and Sami:** Sami is a child refugee from the continuing war in Tarven and Ivo's schoolmate; his house, crooked stairs, and absent grandmother are real memories in the fictional world. He is not a mouthpiece for one Lioran coalition.
- **Joren Fenn:** Independent mechanic, father of Tessa, married to **Mira Fenn**. His programme *The Honest Hour* benefits from Public Voice Foundation support and increasingly rewards outrage. He has real qualms, does not knowingly have a proven foreign handler, and has avoided cameras at Mrs. Kade's memorial. His incomplete apology to Lev did not undo damage at the clinic.
- **Dr. Irena Solvik:** Has a daughter **Elin** and six-year-old granddaughter **Leni**. She has accepted promotion to Deputy Commissioner for Civic Resilience while knowing the department's reported causal success is overstated. She has not yet received the final medal.
- **Niko Rahl:** Lives separately from Irena's family; has a sister whose twelve-year-old son **Pael** receives a bicycle. Niko has insisted that data cannot prove every fracture was caused by Veyr. Do not conflate him with the narrator or make him an omniscient spy.
- **Sela and Lev:** Still married, caring for one another, sometimes strained by Sela's increasing tendency to investigate everything. Sela maintains grounded uncertainty about causes; she is not irrational merely for documenting a possible pattern.
- **Eva Maren and Samuel Rees:** The *Merrow Chronicle* published a careful funding investigation and a later correction; it has real financial risk. Eva has verified payments through Harborbridge to organizations linked with opposing political camps. She has **not** yet established ultimate ownership, a Veyr link, intentional coordination between recipients, or the effects of specific spending.
- **Mrs. Kade:** Died at ninety-one of ordinary age-related illness in May. Her funeral is the last large shared neighborhood Sunday. Dain brings her inscribed childhood book, and the brothers almost agree to go fishing together, but Tomas's old suspicion revives. Their refusal remains their own responsibility.
- **Future risk:** A later revelation must not retroactively make all domestic political disputes fake. Preserve genuine grievances and independent agency while showing how Veyr benefits from fragmentation. The Part IV election, the evidence crossing the sea, later abusive psychiatry, actual loss of sovereignty, and the psychologists' medals remain future plot events.

## Continuity notes after Part IV

- **Election:** Renewal leader **Garran Holt** wins the largest single party tally, but no majority. The coalition led by Common Future's **Nera Valen** forms a **legally valid** government with the Civic Moderates and Free Coast. Verified counting errors and localized misconduct are dealt with through legal channels, not demonstrated to overturn the result. Citizens can dispute politics legitimately; Veyr cannot claim to have determined the vote.
- **Public trust:** Liora's courts, ministries and elections continue operating, but losing factions increasingly refuse to regard government as legitimate. The fracture is political, social and economic—not a fabricated vote count or total collapse yet.
- **Merrow services:** Bakery upstairs meeting room closes for mixed insurance, repair and social reasons. Railway loses skilled workers. Clinic transfers suffer coordination failures. Repair Circle loses lease after a genuine sponsorship dispute. The *Chronicle* downsizes; Eva and Samuel retain scrupulous standards under financial pressure.
- **Dain and Tomas:** They work together on a late-night power/railway incident and restore power to sheltered housing, but revert to old grievances after partisan posts misuse their photograph. They have not reconciled.
- **Children:** Ivo is roughly thirteen. His relationship with Tessa remains strained despite their earlier bridge project and his message about the hinge. He builds a hand-cranked generator with Tarven refugee schoolmate Sami. Tessa is technically skilled and briefly points out a defective bearing. Do not suddenly reconcile them.
- **Northern proof:** Niko Rahl signs evidence concerning the Ministry of Continuity's **Department of Civic Weather**, the **Southern Liaison Program**, Kest's authorization, and a funded relationship with **Harborbridge Civic Services**. Material reaches the south through an independent research organization investigating abuses linked to Tarven. The full chain is not publicly exposed. Niko's identity remains protected publicly, but his access may be investigated by Veyr security.
- **Evidence limits:** The documents show a deliberate program aimed at weakening cross-group civic cooperation, and corroborate certain payment links. They **do not** establish that Veyr scripted Joren's words, that domestic groups knowingly colluded, that every viral event was engineered, or that the election result was rigged. The Ministry denies the materials, without responding to independent corroboration.
- **Publication:** Eva and Samuel print the verified story in the *Merrow Chronicle*. Each political camp circulates selective screenshots implicating the other. Nera Valen announces a broadly tasked inquiry; partisan fights delay appointment of commissioners. The library removes Sela's evidence display table on institutional grounds, though documents remain available on request. Sela is not yet detained.
- **Joren:** He attempts to suspend *The Honest Hour* amid funding doubts, but a producer uses old footage in a fresh, misleading compilation under broad contractual reuse rights. Mira comforts him; his past behavior is not erased.
- **Irena and Kest:** Irena understands the harm and rejects Niko's memorandum rather than standing against ministry policy. Kest begins reviewing whether a self-sustaining civic fracture requires less external funding. Veyr has not received its final medals.
- **Part V handoff:** Real intimidation and public disorder lead Liora's cabinet to consider a public-order emergency bill. A broad **psychiatric risk-assessment** provision enters a later draft and faces legal objection. The **Public Disorder Act** has not yet passed. Later chapters must introduce psychiatrist Dr. Oren Pell, preserve distinction between psychiatric illness and dissent, show corrupted evaluations as abuse, and leave the north's final victory for Part VI.

## Continuity notes after Part V

- **Legal status:** Liora has a legitimate, still-functioning parliament and government led by Nera Valen, but the Public Disorder Act has passed with ambiguous emergency risk clauses. There are legal appeals and real courts; safeguards are undermined by broad exceptions, delay, and administrative framing. Liora remains sovereign at the end of Part V. **Dissolution must occur concretely in Part VI**, not in retrospective summary.
- **Sela Orin:** Taken from her Rowan Way home under an emergency transfer order and confined at **Eastmere Regional Evaluation Centre**, a mixed facility with a designated Public Disorder Act wing and no number on its blue gate. She has never been shown to threaten anyone or exhibit symptoms warranting involuntary psychiatric detention. Her public claims are grounded in the documented Civic Weather / Harborbridge records and she consistently acknowledges uncertainty. Her three sheets of handwritten facts remain with her; her original notebook and phone are stored as property. Her records misrepresent disagreement as lack of insight. Court review requests more information but does not free her.
- **Care and dignity:** Eastmere contains patients with actual psychiatric care needs, including **Lina**, whom Sela befriends. Nurse **Ines** is kind, documents Sela's statements accurately where she can, and understands that ordinary anger at being detained is not a diagnosis. Avoid treating mental illness or care as inherently sinister; the crime is institutional abuse of their authority.
- **Doctors:** **Dr. Oren Pell** assesses Sela thoroughly, finds no clinical basis for detention and signs a written refusal. This costs him a role on the emergency assessment panel and renewal of that contract; he remains a psychiatrist serving genuine patients and keeps an archive of objections with appropriate legal consent. **Dr. Marek Voss**, an experienced but administratively compliant consultant, signs a recommendation for secure evaluation, relying on the distorted referral. Voss is responsible for that choice; he is not evidence that all physicians agree.
- **Lev Orin:** Sela's wife and longtime clinic physician. Refuses to sign a prepared statement retracting legitimate criticism of her wife's case; loses her supervisory role pending standards review, with potential further professional risk. She is not imprisoned. She continues appealing with **Aron Vale**, a lawyer specializing in patient and family rights, and visiting Sela. She keeps Sela's scarf on the crooked coat hook and sets two plates at supper.
- **Eva and Samuel:** The *Merrow Chronicle* continues online at reduced capacity but **suspends print publication** because of printing and credit problems, administrative obstacles, and legal risk. Their northern investigation remains accurate and publicly known; no false retraction. Eva is not yet detained. Public hearings of the government inquiry into northern influence are paused; the underlying evidence remains real.
- **Other detentions:** Separate emergency referrals target people such as an election-contract records officer whose audit questions proved partly founded and a school administrator who corrected false claims about Civic Day. Their proceedings differ; do not collapse all into the same case or claim everyone in psychiatric care is a dissident.
- **Family and objects:** Ivo is in early adolescence, Tomas and Mara still share the house but sleep in separate rooms. Dain has not reconciled with Tomas. Sela's library attendance book, the six original supper tables, the broken/repaired white plastic table, Mrs. Kade's book, coat hook, and the promised fishing trip remain motifs.
- **North:** Niko Rahl is under security clearance review in Veyr, reassigned to an office with no substantive work; no public charge yet. Irena Solvik continues signing misleading successes despite knowing the limits. Director Kest's office now considers Liora's future dependency, cross-border guarantees and the erosion of sovereign decision-making. **No medals have yet been conferred.** Keep final medal ceremony for Chapter 23, and the outcome must be actual dissolution, not only a change of government.
- **Part VI setup:** Chapter 22 should enact the end of sovereignty as a concrete legal and administrative event under domestic paralysis and northern pressure; Chapter 23 honors Veyr's psychologists; Chapter 24 returns to an emptied Rowan Way and Ivo at age seventeen; epilogue juxtaposes award citation with the continuing wrongful confinement and the true record.

## Structural plan

### PART I — THE SUMMER OF OPEN DOORS (published; chapters 01–05)
1. **The Long Table.** A neighborhood supper in Merrow establishes the social life to be lost. The war in distant Tarven remains a news story.
2. **The Department of Civic Weather.** Dr. Solvik receives the Liora brief. The human photograph and statistical abstraction collide.
3. **A Very Ordinary Notification.** Mara's justified campaign for children's access to sport is caught up in a profitable, more aggressive national discussion.
4. **The First Silence.** Dain and Tomas argue and do not apologize. Ivo loses his fishing trip.
5. **The Shape of a Missing Person.** Sela and Eva begin to observe civic withdrawal. Niko sees the same general phenomenon from the northern side.

### PART II — THE NAMES WE GIVE OUR ENEMIES (published; chapters 06–09)
6. **A Useful Kind of Certainty.** Renewal and Common Future take shape; Mara finds solidarity and a new sense of belonging, while Tomas and Dain learn how political names change family obligations.
7. **The Second Table.** Sela assembles a diminished summer supper. Dain returns with burnt peppers, Ivo receives a promised fishing trip, and one brief opportunity for the brothers to reconcile vanishes.
8. **A Man Worth Listening To.** Joren Fenn, a genuine mechanic with legitimate complaints, becomes the voice of the Honest Hour. His new audience and financial sponsors reshape what he is willing to say; Lev sees the clinic suffer.
9. **The Ledger.** Eva verifies documents linking Harborbridge Civic Services to payments associated with both political sides. She has evidence of opaque financing, not proof of Veyr's role. Editor Samuel Rees fears the Chronicle cannot survive a legal fight. Sela's attendance books record a parallel withdrawal from civic life.

### PART III — THE FEED BETWEEN US (published; chapters 10–13)
10. **The Separate Bedrooms.** Mara and Tomas become strangers in a house they still share. When the *Chronicle* publishes Eva's carefully documented funding story, incompatible retellings turn its findings into another marital conflict; Ivo watches his father move rooms.
11. **Children of the Borderless War.** The school's Civic Day collapses under pressure from adult factions. Ivo and Tessa Fenn, who built a working drawbridge together, fall out over their fathers and the threat of public humiliation; Sami from war-torn Tarven gives them a different perspective.
12. **Official Results.** Irena's ministry presentation uses genuine signs of declining trust while overstating what Veyr caused. Niko presses for correction, Irena accepts a promotion, and bureaucratic reward replaces professional caution.
13. **The Last Shared Sunday.** Mrs. Kade dies of old age, not political conflict. At her memorial the town manages to eat, mourn, and listen together. Tomas and Dain almost reconcile, but the old patterns return before either accepts the invitation to go fishing.

### PART IV — THE COUNTRY THAT HATED ITSELF (published; chapters 14–17)
14. **The Election Nobody Could Lose.** Renewal wins the most votes and seats, but a lawful Common Future-led coalition wins parliament's confidence. The electoral process survives formal challenges, while broad social legitimacy disintegrates. Ivo cannot understand why each camp claims a lawful defeat ends the country.
15. **A Republic of Small Rooms.** The upstairs political meeting room, railway depot, clinic coordination, repair circle, library associations, and Chronicle all diminish. Dain and Tomas restore power together during a real outage, but a photograph of the repair re-enters partisan interpretation. Sela adds a notebook page for what still works.
16. **The File That Crossed the Sea.** Niko confronts Irena over the program's ethics, signs limited records documenting Kest's authorization of Civic Weather's covert Southern Liaison Program and a Harborbridge relationship, and passes them to an independent Tarven-focused research organization. Eva verifies elements without claiming the north decided Liora's election. The file carries the two Rowan Way supper photographs.
17. **Every Fact Has Two Histories.** The Chronicle publishes genuine, carefully bounded evidence. Renewal and Common Future circulate opposing crops of the same documents. Joren tries to stop the Honest Hour; the producer reuses earlier material. Sela's library evidence table is removed under institutional pressure. Veyr recognizes domestic actors now sustain much of the fragmentation. Liora's cabinet receives the first draft of emergency public-order powers, including a nascent psychiatric assessment provision.

### PART V — THE DIAGNOSIS (published; chapters 18–21)
18. **The Public Disorder Act.** A real injury at the clinic fuels demand for a public-order law. The new medical risk clauses contain serious ambiguities; Prime Minister Nera Valen votes for the bill despite recognizing that they remain unresolved. Psychiatrist Oren Pell publicly distinguishes mental illness from political disagreement. Sela's lawful library work is referred for assessment.
19. **A Doctor's Signature.** Sela undergoes a voluntary interview with Oren, who finds no clinical basis for involuntary detention and signs his refusal. The emergency assessment panel removes him. Consultant psychiatrist Marek Voss, relying heavily on an administrative framing, signs a contradictory recommendation for secure evaluation.
20. **The House With No Street Number.** Sela is transferred to Eastmere Regional Evaluation Centre under emergency powers, where a file misstates her ordinary, evidence-based beliefs. Her wife Lev and lawyer Aron Vale challenge the order, and the court requests further review without ordering immediate release. A few related public-order referrals target people who raised substantiated concerns. Sela befriends Lina, who has genuine psychiatric care needs.
21. **The Calm Report.** The Chronicle suspends print publication under a convergence of commercial costs, legal risk, and administrative restrictions; its lawful reporting is harder to sustain. Lev refuses to retract her criticism of Sela's detention and loses her supervisory role pending review, but retains her clinical identity. Oren continues treating patients and documents his objection. Sela remains confined. Irena signs a report presenting reduced civic participation as stabilization; Kest begins planning for Liora's formal dependency and eventual loss of sovereignty, which has NOT occurred yet.

### PART VI — THE LAST COUNTRY (planned; chapters 22–24)
22. **The Map With Fewer Names.** Under emergency governance and external pressure, the republic's sovereignty is formally dissolved, its territory reorganized under dependent administrations. This is a concrete event, not a metaphor.
23. **The Medals.** Northern state ceremony. Irena and her peers are publicly honored for strategic achievements under euphemistic names. The state claims the country was saved from itself.
24. **The Empty Table.** Return to Rowan Way. The absence is total but rendered in ordinary objects. Ivo sees the new flag and the old photograph; the land exists, the republic does not.

**Epilogue — The Official Record.** The award citation declares the work complete. In a psychiatric ward, someone who warned the country still waits for her evidence to be heard. No omniscient narrator excuses the winners.

## Writing rules

1. Start scenes with bodily, ordinary experience rather than theory.
2. Let dialogue expose the growing gap between intention and interpretation.
3. No speech about misinformation in place of a story. Show the bill unpaid, the party canceled, the child waiting.
4. Each northern chapter should carry a moment of believable personal ordinariness and a morally chilling bureaucratic decision.
5. Do not include a replicable, individualized real-world targeting manual. The plot concerns consequences and culpability, not a step-by-step instruction book for political manipulation.
6. The reader knows more than most southern characters, but never more than the narrator has earned.
7. Never treat exposure as a reset button. Accurate information may arrive after social capacity to act on it has gone.
8. Track objects across parts: **the six folding tables; the photograph; the coat hook; the snow shovels; the fishing trip; the library attendance book; the award citation**.
9. Show that the aggressor's success depends partly on domestic complicity and ordinary institutions failing, not on perfect mind control.
10. Preserve a hard ending. The northern psychologists get their medals. The country is actually gone. The wrongly institutionalized remain confined.

## Publishing and audio workflow

Each released chapter is a UTF-8 Markdown file under `book/part-XX/`. The JSON manifest lists published chapters, their reading-site URLs, word counts, and eventual audio assets. Future chapters are planned in this document but must not be listed as published in the manifest.

The root `index.html` site uses only static HTML/CSS/JavaScript and works on GitHub Pages with no database, API key, or server. Local Python audio rendering uses Piper, an open-source local TTS engine, and optional FFmpeg. The narrator reads the Markdown text, not a paraphrase. Each chapter can be rendered separately and published as `audio/<chapter-slug>.mp3`. The site checks for audio availability and shows a clear fallback while no audio file exists.
